# Southern Passage: data, papers, and model plan

This is a research and integration guide for SIH26059, not a claim that any forecast or route is operationally validated. The browser's default map and routes remain illustrative; its separate observation mode displays actual NOAA S-NPP VIIRS SIC from the checksummed regional subset described below. Research baselines have been trained from real Antarctic SIC NetCDF data and installed for the API; they are not operational forecasts. A NOAA/NSIDC G02202 v6 archive for 1989-2016 has been acquired, verified against its 28-file SHA-256 manifest, and used for chronological training, a strict 2017-2024 holdout, and ten expanding-window hindcast folds with ground-area-weighted reporting. The model's advantage over persistence remains only about 0.2-0.4% on these retrospective tests, with some regional/seasonal regressions; the limited 2024 VIIRS comparison is not grid-matched. None of this establishes forecast or route skill.

## Initial model selection

`config/initial-model.json` pins the existing one-day `sic-lag-regression-v1` artifact, its full-file SHA-256, coefficient fingerprint, internal artifact digest, the 2017–2024 post-training holdout, and the 2025 challenger comparison. `ml/verify_initial_model.py` checks those bindings, and the API fails closed if the mounted model differs from the pinned choice. This is a reproducible research-model selection, not certification. The model uses two prior daily SIC grids and calendar seasonality to estimate the next day's SIC fraction; it does not use weather, ocean currents, or iceberg motion. The expanded-data candidate was not promoted because its 2025 RMSE reduction versus the current model was just 0.037%, below the 1% screening rule, and its ice-edge disagreement was slightly worse. The 2025 comparison is not a pristine untouched release test. The next candidate must also face prospectively frozen periods, cross-product/grid-aligned testing, uncertainty calibration, and route-scale outcomes before any ministry decision use.

## Matched Copernicus forcing experiment (2026-10-02)

Authenticated official subsets cover 1 June–31 August 2024 in a 10° × 4° Antarctic Peninsula box: [OSI-SAF AMSR2 South daily SIC](https://data.marine.copernicus.eu/product/SEAICE_GLO_SEAICE_L4_NRT_OBSERVATIONS_011_001/services), [GLORYS daily near-surface currents](https://data.marine.copernicus.eu/product/GLOBAL_MULTIYEAR_PHY_001_030/services), and [reprocessed L4 hourly wind](https://data.marine.copernicus.eu/product/WIND_GLO_PHY_L4_MY_012_006/services). A separate [MetOp-C ASCAT daily wind](https://data.marine.copernicus.eu/product/WIND_GLO_PHY_L3_NRT_012_002/services) subset yielded only 79 paired cells on 34 of 92 days in this box; it is not used as full-field forcing. L4 wind provides continuous retrospective coverage but includes a reanalysis/model component.

`ml/evaluate_copernicus_fusion.py` takes the daily mean of hourly wind, interpolates current and wind vectors to the SIC grid without filling missing coastal cells, and uses day-*t* SIC, its one-day change, and day-*t* vector fields to predict day-*t+1* SIC. It trains on 3 June–31 July (102,424 matched pixel-days) and holds out the entire 1–31 August period (53,816 matched pixel-days, 43.4% of the box grid). All comparisons use the exact same valid cells. The [checksummed report](outputs/copernicus-fusion-2024-aug-holdout.json) records fitted coefficients and source hashes. August RMSE/MAE, in SIC percentage points: persistence 3.665/1.313; SIC-lag ridge 3.744/1.336; SIC/current/wind ridge 3.721/1.322. **Neither fitted model beats persistence.** These are spatially correlated pixels in one season, not independent voyages. The report is a retrospective experiment, not a forecast-origin backtest: GLORYS and reprocessed L4 wind may use later information, and source publication times are not verified. It is not promoted to `config/initial-model.json` or exposed as route guidance.

Reproduce after mounting the three subsets:

```powershell
work\convlstm-env\Scripts\python.exe ml\evaluate_copernicus_fusion.py --sic work\datasets\copernicus-osi-sic-south-peninsula-2024-jun-aug\peninsula_20240601_20240831.nc --currents work\datasets\copernicus-glorys-currents-peninsula-2024-jun-aug\peninsula_surface_currents_20240601_20240831.nc --wind work\datasets\copernicus-l4-wind-peninsula-2024-jun-aug\peninsula_hourly_wind_20240601_20240831.nc --split 2024-08-01 --output outputs\copernicus-fusion-2024-aug-holdout.json
```

## Recommended staged stack

1. **Observations and hindcasts:** start with the NOAA/NSIDC passive-microwave concentration record, split by date into train/validation/test, and add motion and forcing only after spatial/time alignment and quality checks. OSI-455 and NSIDC-0116 have both been acquired for retrospective experiments; the latter has a checksummed 2023–2024 cross-year test below. Preserve source files, checksums, metadata, projection, and attribution.
2. **Forecast baseline:** train a compact, interpretable gridded lag-regression baseline (next-day concentration from recent concentration and seasonal phase). Benchmark against persistence and a day-of-year climatology with rolling-origin splits. It is a research baseline, not a substitute for an Antarctic operational forecast system.
3. **Iceberg drift:** use observed BYU/NIC tracks for hindcast evaluation and OpenDrift's OpenBerg physics model with explicit wind/current forcing. It is not a pretrained predictor; OpenDrift is GPL-2.0, so ministry deployment/distribution needs a license review.
4. **Route support:** keep routing as an independently testable, explainable constraint/cost engine. Require authoritative coastline, bathymetry, ice chart, ship capability, under-keel clearance, weather/sea state, and approved safety rules before any operational use. A sea-ice concentration field alone does not mean navigable water.

## OSI-455 motion-advection diagnostic

`ml/fetch_osi455.py` downloads bounded Southern Hemisphere date windows from the public MET Norway THREDDS archive. It supports limited parallel transfers and writes atomic incomplete checkpoints so interrupted pulls can resume only after matching each cached source URL, byte length, and SHA-256; the final manifest also records the product metadata and structural validation. The `ml/evaluate_osi455_advection.py` diagnostic pairs date-labeled G02202 v6 concentration D with D+1 concentration and the latest complete OSI motion interval before the experiment's assumed 00Z issue label: OSI file D-1 ends at 12Z D-1. This avoids post-label vector leakage, but does not prove strict operational causality: the [NSIDC G02202 v6 User Guide](https://nsidc.org/sites/default/files/documents/user-guide/g02202-v006-userguide.pdf) describes G02202 as a daily product and its midnight coordinate as time in days from an epoch, not as a known publication/availability timestamp. It area-averages SIC onto the 75-km OSI grid, excludes G02202 missing/invalid/spatially interpolated cells and every temporal interpolation, and accepts OSI flags 21 or 30 only. It fits one shrinkage factor on the first half of a fit period and can apply that coefficient unchanged to later evaluation dates via `--fixed-lambda`. The report includes monthly strict-motion and common valid-cell coverage, so zero-data days cannot silently disappear from the score. `ml/summarize_osi455_hindcasts.py` combines frozen-coefficient year reports while retaining equal-year and sample-weighted scores and coverage. Install the pinned optional dependencies from `requirements-motion-research.txt`.

Fitting lambda=0.1 on the first half of September 2017 and freezing it for all September dates in 2018, 2019 and 2020 changed sample-weighted MAE from 0.015136 for persistence to 0.015088 (0.32% reduction) and RMSE from 0.025519 to 0.025390 (0.51%). The total is 221,824 correlated grid-cell/day values; equal-year mean MAE reduction is 0.32%. Per-year results and input/manifest hashes are in `outputs/osi455-september-2017-fit-through-2020.json`; the fit, validation and individual evaluation outputs are preserved beside it. This is a multi-year same-season retrospective diagnostic, but the small gain does not establish meaningful forecast skill or justify integration. A separate within-Sep-2020 experiment (lambda=0.1 trained on its first half, tested on its second) is also recorded in `outputs/osi455-sep2020-advection.json`. A January 2020 sample tests austral-summer coverage: across the acquired 31 motion days, only 11,430 of 642,816 grid-days have any success flag 20-30 (1.78%), exclusively OSI status 23/24 wind-parameter gap-filled or wind-replaced vectors. No cells pass the stricter 21/30 screen, so the evaluator refuses to score the period instead of presenting wind-filled values as observed drift. This flags a seasonal coverage limitation; it does not prove the product is generally unusable. The OSI vectors precede an assumed midnight date label, but G02202's daily date coordinate does not establish the input field's actual availability time; this pairing is not proof of operational causality. No motion inputs have been added to or used to retrain the packaged SIC model.

## NSIDC-0116 motion alignment and retrospective hindcast

A real-file smoke check found that NSIDC's source y axis increases while G02202's target y decreases. ## Current route-exposure API

`POST /api/v1/route/exposure` is a deliberately narrow analytical endpoint, not a route planner. It accepts a WGS84 GeoJSON 2D `LineString`, date, and optional `analysis_half_width_km` (0–20 km per side; default 5 km). The service reads the checksummed local VIIRS scene and samples nearest pixel centers along the geodesic centerline and, when width is positive, across a geodesic context band; spacing is at most half a pixel. Routes over 1,000 km and requests with invalid coordinates are rejected. The response reports source/date provenance, centerline statistics, band mean/max and sample coverage, projected centerline and swath geometry for map overlay, and 5-km profiles with explicit coverage. Width zero preserves centerline-only analysis. The web screen allows 2–500 longitude/latitude waypoints and selectable widths. This fixed-width swath is not a vessel-specific navigation corridor or clearance assessment. Neither screen nor endpoint assesses data quality sufficiently for operations, ice drift, weather, currents, bathymetry, ship class, charts, legal restrictions, or navigability. A human-validated operational voyage-support product requires those authoritative feeds and approved agency safety rules, plus validation by polar scientists and maritime operators.

The acquired 2024-06-04 VIIRS scene is a useful service smoke-test fixture: it has 1,530 non-missing cells on its 253×253 regional grid. A short route deliberately placed through one valid pixel returned a 200 response with 3/4 centerline samples covered and a mean SIC fraction of 0.979 for those observed samples. On 2026-09-29, the observed-data web flow was also exercised in a browser: it loaded the scene, accepted two WGS84 waypoints, and rendered the 1.0-km result, 75% coverage, summary, and source provenance. The pinned Docker image was built and run temporarily in production mode: readiness became healthy, an unauthenticated catalog request returned 401, and authenticated catalog, scene and route requests succeeded with the same partial-coverage result. This validates packaging, credential enforcement and one data path only; one date and one tiny route do not establish general coverage, scientific skill, or route safety. The service may correctly return partial or zero coverage elsewhere in the regional subset. Vulnerability/license scanning and ministry-specific security review remain outstanding.

## Historical iceberg tracks API

`GET /api/v1/icebergs/observed-tracks?as_of_date=YYYY-MM-DD&window_days=30` serves a GeoJSON `FeatureCollection` of BYU/NIC v8.0 source-measured large-iceberg positions in the requested trailing window. It excludes rows marked interpolated, averages multiple source-sensor positions for the same iceberg/day, reports each track's last observed date and age, and validates the archive against its sibling manifest before caching the parsed file. The bundled local archive SHA-256 is `47d899c8bb1c3266d0b8c94a6f87c435dc68827a53f39dea3d78fe68ccc40cae`. The API caps the window at 90 days and refuses dates after the BYU landing page's stated coverage endpoint of 2025-04-22; later records present in the local archive are not served pending source clarification. Set `SOUTHERN_PASSAGE_ICEBERG_TRACKS` to the ZIP path in other deployments. Docker Compose uses a read-only mount. The interface now displays up to three latest source-measured positions in its historical-track panel, queried over the 90 days ending at the published coverage cutoff; it does not place them on the schematic map or claim they are near the illustrative passage. The feed remains archival, covers known large bergs only, and is not current positions, a complete census, predicted drift, or encounter-risk output. Its integrity/coverage tests pass, and the image previously served 39 features from the actual mounted archive in a temporary localhost-only container; none of this validates the tracks as a route-hazard input.

## Observed-data one-day estimate

`GET /api/v1/observations/sic/{date}/forecast` provides a one-day lag-regression estimate directly from mounted data, avoiding caller-entered source claims. It requires the selected initialization scene and immediately preceding calendar day to exist, match in product/platform/CRS/grid, and have at least 10% common valid pixels; both files already passed the catalog's manifest SHA-256 verification. The 10% threshold is only a demo rendering floor—not an approved quality/operational threshold—and does not establish spatial uniformity. It returns source filename and checksum for both inputs, target date, coefficient and full-artifact identities, the same-product holdout and limited regional VIIRS transfer check, common-pixel count/coverage, and `decision_status: research_only`. The observed-data page has a “Preview next day” action that paints the estimate on the same VIIRS grid and hides route analysis until the user returns to the observation. The mounted 2024 subset contains 75 dates through 2024-08-31, so only dates with a consecutive prior scene can initialize it; the newest pair has only 2 valid cells in common and correctly fails the display floor. This is one-day SIC estimation only—not navigation guidance; the regional model-versus-persistence difference is small, uncertainty is not calibrated, and there is no operational validation.

## Training plan: what to train, and what not to claim

The existing locally trained model is a low-capacity one-day SIC regression baseline. It is useful as a reproducibility and benchmark fixture, but its negligible multi-year improvement over persistence and poor VIIRS transfer mean it is not a ministry decision model. The 2017–2024 period is a post-training holdout and must stay frozen for model selection. Do not tune to it and then continue calling it independent validation.

For the next model experiment, first lock task/horizon and an operationally relevant Antarctic region with the ministry/scientific owner. Then assemble aligned inputs: G02202 as the long historical target; NSIDC-0116 ice-motion vectors for advection; ERA5 wind and temperature; Copernicus Marine currents/SST; grid/mask/quality flags; and held-out VIIRS/SAR or expert ice charts where licensing and labels permit. Add predictors only where their historical timestamps and spatial grids truly align. For a 1–7-day SIC outlook, compare (1) persistence and seasonal climatology, (2) a motion-advection baseline, (3) the current lag regression, and then (4) a small spatial-temporal CNN/ConvLSTM with masked loss and a probabilistic/ensemble output. Fit against strictly chronological rolling-origin folds, with recent extreme years and VIIRS as untouched independent checks. Report per-lead and per-region area-weighted MAE/RMSE, ice-edge displacement/IIEE, calibration/coverage of uncertainty, missing-data behavior, and skill relative to persistence. Promote a neural candidate only if it consistently beats all baselines on frozen, geographically and temporally appropriate tests and the results are independently reproduced. Track data/model/code hashes, preprocessing, environment, training parameters, evaluation reports, approval and rollback version for every artifact.

The published Antarctic ConvLSTM checkpoint, Ice-kNN-South results and ANTSIC-UNet paper are research references, not known production-ready pretrained integrations. In this workspace the ConvLSTM's exact normalization and full training source are missing and approximate transfer was catastrophically worse than persistence; Ice-kNN-South publishes evaluation products but not a callable inference implementation; ANTSIC-UNet is a seasonal multi-input method with no linked ready-to-run weights found in its paper. Newer public Sentinel-1 iceberg inventories materially improve the candidate data landscape: the Chen et al. 2018-2023 inventory can inform annual spatial exposure, while Jiao et al.'s 2025 grounded-iceberg map and MIT workflow offer a demo model/pipeline for a static coastal-hazard layer. Neither provides a continuous drift model; the latter's grounded/fast-ice distinction and dataset terms require independent review. Do not wrap these into the ministry service until the authors' exact code/data/preprocessing, license, reproducibility, and target-horizon suitability are verified. OpenDrift is an executable physics framework rather than a pretrained predictor; it needs validated forcing and iceberg properties and GPL-2.0 compatibility review before bundling. The appropriate near-term integration is data provenance and offline hindcast tooling, not an unvalidated model endpoint.

## Research and open-source tools reviewed

- The closest task/hemisphere match is *Antarctic sea ice prediction with a convolutional long short-term memory network* (Ocean Modelling, 2024, DOI [10.1016/j.ocemod.2024.102386](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ocemod.2024.102386); [author preprint](https://d197for5662m48.cloudfront.net/documents/publicationstatus/144072/preprint_pdf/39ff19efa8089b2290e4782ba8e69505.pdf)). The preprint describes daily Antarctic SIC inputs from NSIDC-0051/0081, 1989-2016 training, a 2018-2022 evaluation period, regridding to 100 km, six input channels, 90-day sequences and one-day autoregressive rollout. The separately published checkpoint archive below is promising, but is not a deployable service; reproduce its preprocessing and results before relying on it.
- **A genuine Antarctic pretrained candidate does exist:** the study's [Zenodo v2 artifact](https://zenodo.org/records/11234961) contains network weights, model design and paper test data; the record declares CC BY 4.0. We retrieved it, verified the published MD5, inspected the NetCDF schema and safely loaded the state dictionary. The paper trained on 1989–2016 and reported season-dependent daily SIC/ice-edge skill through about one month. The 11 MB archive is not a served model/API; its original multi-decadal training files and complete inference preprocessing are not included. The checkpoint is not yet connected to the product. Its hindcast result is not a live forecast guarantee, and it predicts SIC only—not iceberg drift or route safety. See the local provenance manifest under `work/datasets/convlstm-paper/ConvLSTM/manifest.json`; attribute the authors and license if reusing.
- **Published-checkpoint compatibility probe (2026-09-29):** `ml/evaluate_paper_convlstm.py` verifies and loads the released checkpoint, aligns G02202 25-km SIC to its 100-km test grid, and compares selected one-day forecasts over the bundled 2017-2022 test period with persistence. With approximate training-period Gaussian scaling and G02202-derived day-of-year climatology/std, the eight-origin mean area-weighted RMSE was 0.346 for ConvLSTM vs 0.0226 for persistence. These values are *not valid model-skill evidence*: the released bundle omits exact normalization/inference preprocessing and original training files; G02202 is a distinct product but shares passive-microwave observation lineage with the paper's NSIDC-0051/0081 inputs ([G02202 v6 record](https://nsidc.org/data/g02202/versions/6), [G02202 v6 guide](https://nsidc.org/sites/default/files/documents/user-guide/g02202-v006-userguide.pdf), [NSIDC-0051 v2 record](https://nsidc.org/data/nsidc-0051/versions/2)). Treat this as an unresolved preprocessing-compatibility failure signal, not an independent-source evaluation. Keep the model out of the service; do not train on or tune against the paper's held-out test set. The result JSON explicitly encodes `valid_model_skill_evidence: false`: `outputs/paper-convlstm-transfer.json`.
- **OSI-455 sea-ice drift CDR:** Down et al. (2023), [Earth System Science Data paper](https://essd.copernicus.org/articles/15/5807/2023/), documents a daily global 1991-2020, 75 km OSI SAF drift record. The merged Antarctic component includes 24-hour displacement and uncertainty; satellite gaps/summer are partly represented with a wind-driven free-drift model and spatial interpolation. The complete 2017-2020 archive is checksummed locally. A coefficient fit on the first half of 2017 was frozen for 2018-2020; it reduces SIC MAE by 0.343% and RMSE by 0.720% on 904,107 correlated cell-days, with strict-quality motion coverage on only April-October dates. This weak, non-route-scale result does not support model integration; coarse resolution, model-filled areas, seasonal gaps and unproven daily-field availability remain limitations.
- **Operational short-lead design reference:** Kvanum et al., *Developing a deep learning forecasting system for short-term and high-resolution prediction of sea ice concentration* (The Cryosphere, 2025, [article](https://tc.copernicus.org/articles/19/4149/2025/)). It describes 1 km, 1–3 day forecasts operationalized for East Greenland/Barents using ice charts and weather predictors. This provides strong system/evaluation lessons, but its Arctic-region data/model are not transferable weights for Antarctica.
- **Ice-kNN-South (new Antarctic benchmark):** Lin et al. (2025), [JGR: Machine Learning and Computation paper](https://doi.org/10.1029/2024JH000433), predicts Antarctic SIC anomalies through 90 days using multi-scale field-to-field and grid-to-grid kNN without autoregressive rollout. Its [CC-BY-4.0 Zenodo release](https://zenodo.org/records/13144175) contains verification products and a `verification.py` scorer, but not the model training/inference implementation or spatial forecast grids; it is not a drop-in integration. `ml/fetch_iceknn_verification.py` downloads only the 4.5 MB year-sample archive (not the 2.56 GB full results bundle), and `ml/analyze_iceknn_verification.py` summarizes its released 2023-24 scalar metrics. In that archive, mean IIEE (million km²) is worse than persistence at day 1 (0.457 vs 0.309), but lower at day 30 (2.222 vs 2.595) and day 90 (2.489 vs 3.493); pan-Antarctic mean absolute SIE error similarly shifts from 0.140 vs 0.078 at day 1 to 1.536 vs 1.614 at day 30 and 1.786 vs 2.474 at day 90. These are the authors' aggregate archive results (546 complete initialization cases at day 90), not a local model reproduction or route-scale skill validation. Full provenance and per-lead metrics are in `outputs/ice-knn-south-verification-2023-2024.json`.
- **BYU/NIC iceberg drift baseline experiment:** the v8.0 consolidated archive was checksum-verified locally (4,065,659 bytes; SHA-256 recorded in `work/datasets/byu-iceberg-v8/manifest.json`). `ml/evaluate_iceberg_tracks.py` reads per-sensor positions, excludes interpolation-marker `1` rows (BYU's own `consolplot.m` labels these as interpolated), and fits a single pooled velocity damping coefficient through 2016. The frozen next-report test (2017-01-01 through the landing-page-declared 2025-04-22) has 90,073 examples from 145 iceberg tracks. One-to-two-day constant-velocity extrapolation lowers mean position error from 3.100 km for persistence to 2.497 km, but at 3-7 days errors increase from 14.175 to 20.386 km; at 8-14 days they increase from 30.684 to 36.208 km. The train-fitted damping is zero, so the fitted model reduces to persistence. Errors are serially correlated, iceberg IDs overlap the chronological splits (continuation of known tracks, not unseen-berg generalization), and the paper cites sensor location accuracy of roughly one pixel (2.2-8.9 km depending on sensor), comparable to some reported errors. These are not fixed-horizon or encounter-risk forecasts; no model is promoted. The downloaded archive contains dates through 2026-04-29 despite the landing page's stated April 2025 endpoint, so later dates are excluded pending source clarification. Reproduce with `ml/evaluate_iceberg_tracks.py work/datasets/byu-iceberg-v8/consolidated_database_v8.0.zip --output outputs/byu-iceberg-v8-drift-holdout.json`.

To assess whether an uncertainty radius could be attached to those next-report baselines, `ml/calibrate_iceberg_error_corridors.py` estimates empirical 80%/90% radial position-error quantiles on 2014-2016, after fitting velocity damping only through 2013, then measures coverage on the separate 2017-2025-04-22 period. The 1-2 day constant-velocity 90% radius is 10.05 km and covers 94.2% of held-out reports; persistence uses an 11.81 km radius and covers 93.5%. At 3-7 days, persistence's 33.15 km calibration radius covers 87.5% (below nominal 90%); at 8-14 days its 57.53 km radius covers 85.6%. Constant velocity reaches 91.2% coverage at 8-14 days only with a much larger 99.54 km radius, while its point-error baseline is worse than persistence. These are empirical next-report distance distributions, not formal conformal guarantees: observations are serially dependent, sensor location uncertainty is several kilometres, and no route encounter/collision metric is measured. The bands cannot be treated as live iceberg ellipses or safe-clearance distances. The full manifest-bound report is `outputs/byu-iceberg-v8-error-corridors.json`; reproduce with `ml/calibrate_iceberg_error_corridors.py work/datasets/byu-iceberg-v8/consolidated_database_v8.0.zip --fit-through 2013-12-31 --calibration-start 2014-01-01 --calibration-end 2016-12-31 --test-start 2017-01-01 --test-end 2025-04-22 --output outputs/byu-iceberg-v8-error-corridors.json`.
- **ANTSIC-UNet**: Yang et al. (2025), *Extended seasonal prediction of Antarctic sea ice concentration using ANTSIC-UNet*, [The Cryosphere](https://tc.copernicus.org/articles/19/6381/2025/). This 20-member U-Net ensemble uses a 57-channel monthly input assembled from the prior 12 months of SIC, a six-month linear-trend forecast, ERA5/ORAS5 climate fields and a land mask; it predicts six future monthly SIC maps on the NSIDC 25-km grid. The paper reports skill versus trend, anomaly-persistence and SEAS5, but its horizon and monthly data pipeline differ from a 3-7 day voyage outlook. It links the underlying SIC, ERA5 and ORAS5 datasets, but the article has no code-availability statement or linked released weights/inference repository; this is a reproducibility target, not an installable tool. The paper is open access under CC BY 4.0; that does not grant rights to unavailable model code or input products beyond their own terms.
- **SIPN South**: Massonnet et al. (2023), *SIPN South: six years of coordinated seasonal Antarctic sea ice predictions*, [Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/marine-science/articles/10.3389/fmars.2023.1148899/full). Useful benchmark and shared evaluation context; includes approaches such as ConvLSTM and multivariate statistical models, not a supported operational API.
- **IceNet**: Andersson et al., *Seasonal Arctic sea ice forecasting with probabilistic deep learning*, Nature Communications (2021), [paper](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-021-25257-4), [code/data instructions](https://github.com/tom-andersson/icenet-paper). Excellent open research implementation and reproducibility reference, but trained/validated for the Arctic and seasonal horizons, not a drop-in Antarctic 3–7-day ship-routing model. Check upstream license and all data licences before reuse.
- **Iceberg track source**: Budge & Long (2018), *A Comprehensive Database for Antarctic Iceberg Tracking Using Scatterometer Data*, [paper](https://doi.org/10.1109/JSTARS.2017.2784186), [database](https://www.scp.byu.edu/data/iceberg/database1_recent.html). Provides historic tracks for evaluation; it does not itself forecast drift.
- **OpenDrift/OpenBerg**: [source](https://github.com/OpenDrift/opendrift), [OpenBerg example](https://opendrift.github.io/gallery/example_openberg.html). Open physics-based trajectory framework with an iceberg model. Requires suitable wind/current forcing, iceberg properties, calibration, and hindcast evaluation; repository declares GPL-2.0, which needs legal review for the ministry's deployment model.
- **Scientific data plumbing**: xarray + NetCDF4 for gridded data, Zarr for cloud-optimized derived cubes, DVC or lakeFS for versioned datasets, MLflow for experiment/model registry, FastAPI or an equivalent authenticated service, and STAC/OGC API records for geospatial discovery. These are candidates, not ministry-approved choices; assess hosting, security, support, and license posture before adoption.

## Training and acceptance gates

For replayability, `ml/train_sic.py` writes the requested validation fraction and effective chronological cutoff, fixed regularization/feature settings, source-manifest digest, trainer digest, and runtime versions into each model artifact. It writes a full-artifact SHA-256 distinct from the stable coefficient fingerprint; the API verifies this digest before serving a model and uses the coefficient fingerprint to bind evaluation reports. It replaces artifacts atomically so interrupted writes do not leave a partial JSON model. Small floating-point solver differences may still occur across BLAS/platforms; compare full metrics within a documented tolerance and verify the coefficient fingerprint rather than assuming byte-identical outputs.

The included `ml/train_sic.py` is an initial gridded statistical baseline trainer, not a deep-learning model. It streams daily fields from daily or time-stacked NetCDF files and writes model weights plus a chronological holdout report, including a monthly-mean climatology comparator. For G02202 inputs, training and evaluation are gated on a complete SHA-256 manifest whose entries exactly match the annual files on disk. `ml/fetch_g02202.py` retrieves official NOAA/NSIDC annual Antarctic aggregates in resumable byte ranges and writes that manifest. `ml/fetch_polarwatch_viirs.py` requests a bounded Antarctic ROI from NOAA PolarWatch ERDDAP in small, individually checksummed NetCDF chunks. `ml/evaluate_sic.py` evaluates an existing artifact on a separate source/product, carries through its local checksum manifest, reports overall and target-month metrics, and optionally provides a clearly labelled simple block-mean grid-scale diagnostic. `ml/ice_motion.py` implements and unit-tests NSIDC's Southern Hemisphere along-grid-to-east/north rotation and conservative fill/coastline/distant-source masking; it intentionally does not download or reproject data, so it is not yet part of a trained model. The original paper-test-period baseline was fit on 6.79 million ocean grid/time samples from 2017-01-03 through 2020-08-06 and validated on 4.54 million samples from 2020-08-07 through 2022-12-31. Its ocean-cell RMSE is 0.02380 SIC fraction versus persistence at 0.02432 and monthly-mean climatology at 0.11347; MAE is 0.00873. These figures are from one archived product/period and a single chronological split; they do not establish skill for another data product, location, season, or live forecast, and the original released paper checkpoint was not used in this baseline. The exact dataset/file hashes and split are embedded in `models/sic_baseline.json` and the local manifest. For the multi-decadal model, the expanding-window evaluator now adds G02202 ground-area-weighted and broad latitude-band summaries using the product's polar projection. Remaining gates: evaluate persistence/climatology, lag-regression and suitable dynamical/neural methods at the intended forecast leads; add motion and atmospheric/ocean forcing only after quality-controlled temporal/spatial alignment; report regional ice-edge metrics and calibrated uncertainty; test sensor transitions/missingness; and obtain Antarctic sea-ice scientist and maritime-safety review. Never random-shuffle daily maps across train/test because that leaks adjacent temporal/spatial structure.

A separate one-year artifact, `models/sic_g02202_2010.json`, is fit on the first 70% of 2010 and validated on the final 30% after QA masking (14.3 million vs 6.85 million valid grid samples). Holdout RMSE is 0.04240 vs persistence 0.04268; the monthly climatology is based on fewer validation samples because late-year months are absent from the fit. Transferred to VIIRS June–August 2024 it is nearly persistence at native and approximate 25 km scales.

The expanded quality-filtered artifact, `models/sic_g02202_2010_2014.json`, is trained on five official G02202 annual files: 74.7 million fit samples through 2013-07-01, then a chronological holdout from 2013-07-02 through 2014-12-31 (33.2 million samples). Its holdout RMSE 0.04331 is only marginally better than persistence at 0.04347. On VIIRS June–August 2024 it is effectively tied with persistence: native 795 m RMSE 0.11580 vs 0.11590; approximate 24.645 km block-mean RMSE 0.04936 vs 0.04939. The cross-product period and ROI remain narrow, samples are correlated, and the block averaging is not reprojection; these results do not support operational use. The trainer derives validation-scope text from the selected dates and source manifest. Each trained artifact and evaluation report includes a deterministic coefficient fingerprint; stale evaluation reports are not attached to a different coefficient set by the API. More years, rolling-origin evaluation and properly aligned regional tests remain necessary.

## Strict post-training same-product holdout

To test temporal generalization beyond the fit/validation years, official NOAA/NSIDC G02202 v6 Antarctic annual aggregates for 2017-2024 were acquired separately into `work/datasets/g02202-v6-post2016/`. Each file has a SHA-256 and byte count in its complete manifest. The model was not retrained or tuned on these years. Cell-pooled RMSE across 172.1 million valid cell samples is 0.041192 vs persistence RMSE 0.041281 (about 0.22% lower); MAE is 0.01416. It is better than persistence in each annual aggregate, but regresses slightly in April-August. Because this is EPSG:3412 polar stereographic, true ground cell areas vary with latitude. The report therefore also estimates area weights from projected pixel area divided by PROJ's ellipsoidal areal scale at each cell centre: area-weighted RMSE is 0.041849 vs persistence 0.041944, and area-weighted MAE is 0.01461. In the north-of-60°S band it loses slightly (0.02362 vs 0.02352 RMSE), while the two southern bands show small gains. This centre-scale area estimate is appropriate for broad spatial comparison but is not a geodesic integration of each cell boundary. Neither metric establishes operational or route-safety utility. Counts pool adjacent days and neighboring cells and are not independent sample counts. Full month/year/latitude scores, model fingerprint and source inventory are in `outputs/g02202-2017-2024-holdout.json`. The API exposes it under `same_product_holdout`, separately from the different-sensor VIIRS transfer report.

Using the same 15% SIC extent threshold and area-weight method, 2017-2024 mean daily pan-Antarctic IIEE is 0.325542 million km² vs persistence 0.325353 (slightly worse); mean absolute SIE error is 0.092901 vs 0.092976 (slightly better). These mixed, tiny differences are not route-scale diagnostics. The chosen comparable-holdout endpoint stops in 2024 because NSIDC documents AMSR2 as a new input beginning 1 January 2025. The separate 2025 sensor-era check is above; future years should be evaluated separately, not silently pooled into the pre-2025 holdout. See the [official G02202 v6 record](https://nsidc.org/data/g02202/versions/6). Remaining evaluation needs include route-corridor/region diagnostics, uncertainty calibration, and expert review.

### Frozen recursive multi-lead diagnostic

`ml/evaluate_multilead_sic.py` repeatedly applies the packaged one-day lag-regression coefficients without refitting, using the 2017-2024 complete, manifest-verified G02202 archive. It resets across date gaps, applies the source QA/ocean masks, and scores only target cells with valid origin inputs and target observations. The 1-, 3-, 5-, and 7-day area-weighted RMSEs are 0.04185, 0.07068, 0.08868, and 0.10257 versus persistence at 0.04194, 0.07147, 0.09031, and 0.10518. However, area-weighted MAE is worse than persistence at every lead (0.01461/0.01282, 0.02766/0.02316, 0.03704/0.02986, and 0.04512/0.03533). At the 15% threshold, mean daily IIEE is effectively tied (model vs persistence: 0.3269/0.3267, 0.6540/0.6541, 0.8861/0.8867, 1.0809/1.0821 million km²). Each lead has about 2,896-2,908 target days, but adjacent days and cells are correlated. This is a frozen same-product hindcast, not independent sensor transfer, uncertainty calibration, route-scale evaluation, or an operational forecast. The mixed metrics do not justify connecting the recursive outputs to the UI; its +3/+5/+7 scenario remains illustrative. Full provenance and per-lead metrics are in `outputs/g02202-2017-2024-recursive-multilead.json`. Reproduce with `ml/evaluate_multilead_sic.py work/datasets/g02202-v6-post2016 --model models/sic_g02202_1989_2016.json --output outputs/g02202-2017-2024-recursive-multilead.json`.

The multi-decadal artifact, `models/sic_g02202_1989_2016.json`, uses 28 annual G02202 v6 files spanning 1989-2016. Each file is listed by name, byte length and SHA-256 in `work/datasets/g02202-v6/south/manifest.json`; trainer/evaluator verify the complete manifest and file hashes before use. The chronological 20% holdout contains 121.8 million grid-cell samples; RMSE is 0.042622 vs persistence 0.042676 (MAE 0.01530; monthly-mean climatology RMSE 0.15141). This tiny improvement over persistence is not compelling on its own. On the separate 2024 VIIRS ROI (122,771 correlated native-grid samples), RMSE is 0.11261 vs persistence 0.11590; this is one sparse, 100-km regional subset on a different sensor/grid and is a transfer diagnostic only—not robust independent validation or proof of navigational utility. Reports are in `outputs/polarwatch-viirs-g02202-1989-2016.json`.

### Direct-horizon SIC regression experiment

`ml/evaluate_direct_multilead_sic.py` fits a separate small ridge regression for each requested lead (default 1/3/5/7 days), using the latest two *observed* SIC fields at forecast origin plus target-day seasonality. It fits only through 2016-12-31, verifies both complete G02202 source manifests and each file hash, then scores the frozen coefficients on 2017-2024; unlike recursive rollout, predicted fields are never fed back as model inputs. Estimated ground-area-weighted RMSE (direct model vs persistence) is 0.04183/0.04194, 0.07068/0.07147, 0.08864/0.09031, and 0.10250/0.10518 at 1/3/5/7 days. Area-weighted MAE remains worse at every lead: 0.01456/0.01282, 0.02760/0.02316, 0.03672/0.02986, 0.04444/0.03533. Stratification by target month, year and latitude is included; latitude bands without valid target cells are explicitly reported as unscored (the south-of-80°S band has no valid target cells in this run). At 7 days the model loses north of 60°S (area-weighted RMSE 0.05737 vs persistence 0.05614; MAE 0.02216 vs 0.00872), while the two populated southern latitude bands have lower RMSE but still worse MAE. The pan-Antarctic RMSE gain therefore conceals a regional regression. This does not support promotion: it is same-product evaluation with strongly correlated dates/cells, no route-corridor score, no independent sensor confirmation for these horizons, and no calibrated uncertainty. The model is archived as `models/sic_direct_multilead.json`; coefficients are fingerprinted and the artifact digest binds its provenance. The full per-lead and stratified report is `outputs/sic-direct-multilead-holdout.json`. Reproduce with `ml/evaluate_direct_multilead_sic.py work/datasets/g02202-v6/south work/datasets/g02202-v6-post2016 --cutoff 2016-12-31 --model-output models/sic_direct_multilead.json --report-output outputs/sic-direct-multilead-holdout.json`. To rescore the exact archived coefficients without refitting, add `--model-input models/sic_direct_multilead.json`. The app remains illustrative for multi-day scenarios.

To test whether a useful fraction of the direct model could be retained without sacrificing MAE, `ml/calibrate_sic_persistence_blend.py` fits the direct regressions through 2014, chooses a convex model/persistence blend by area-weighted absolute-error minimization on 2015-2016, then evaluates on the untouched 2017-2024 holdout. The weighted-median calibration selected alpha=0.0 at every lead (1/3/5/7 days), so persistence is the MAE-minimizing choice on the calibration period; the frozen holdout blend consequently matches persistence within floating-point precision. This is evidence against promoting the current regression, not evidence that persistence is an adequate navigation forecast. The calibrated artifact and report are `models/sic_direct_multilead_blended.json` and `outputs/sic-direct-multilead-blended-holdout.json`. Reproduce with `ml/calibrate_sic_persistence_blend.py work/datasets/g02202-v6/south work/datasets/g02202-v6-post2016 --fit-cutoff 2014-12-31 --calibration-start 2015-01-01 --calibration-end 2016-12-31 --leads 1,3,5,7 --model-output models/sic_direct_multilead_blended.json --report-output outputs/sic-direct-multilead-blended-holdout.json`. The report includes both calibration-period selection scores and the separate frozen holdout, with the selection caveat explicit.

To probe temporal stability, `ml/rolling_evaluate_sic.py` streamed the same manifest-verified archive through ten expanding-window folds (8 initial training years, then successive disjoint 2-year validation blocks from 1997-1998 through 2015-2016). Across 430.6 million valid grid-cell samples, pooled RMSE is 0.043910 vs persistence 0.044072; estimated ground-area-weighted RMSE is 0.044538 vs 0.044706 over the same folds. Both improvements are only about 0.4%. The area-weighted model regresses slightly north of 60°S (0.027805 vs 0.027788 RMSE) and in May-June; other broad bands show small gains. Weights estimate each cell's ground area using projected pixel area divided by the projection's ellipsoidal areal scale at the cell centre; they are not a geodesic integration of cell boundaries. The temporal folds share historical training data, and cell-time samples are correlated, so this does not establish independent or route-scale skill. The full per-fold, monthly and latitude-band report is `outputs/g02202-1989-2016-rolling-areaweighted.json`. No model from this work should be presented as an operational forecast.

The updated report, `outputs/g02202-1989-2016-rolling-climatology.json`, additionally scores a per-pixel calendar-month mean fitted only on each fold's earlier training dates. Across the same ten folds and 430.6 million shared samples, the lag regression is 0.043910 cell-pooled RMSE and 0.044538 estimated area-weighted RMSE; persistence is 0.044072 and 0.044706, respectively; the monthly-mean climatology is 0.153415 and 0.155889. This simple fixed seasonal mean is a deliberately limited reference and performs poorly because it cannot condition on current ice state; it is not a seasonal-anomaly-persistence or a full operational seasonal forecast. The regression's roughly 0.4% advantage over persistence is still small and not route-scale evidence. Reproduce the expanded report with `ml/rolling_evaluate_sic.py work/datasets/g02202-v6/south --train-years 8 --validation-years 2 --step-years 2 --output outputs/g02202-1989-2016-rolling-climatology.json`.

When recognized G02202 QA is present, the reader masks cells marked with QA bits 8 (no input brightness temperatures), 16 (invalid ice/ocean mask) and 64 (temporal interpolation) on all daily inputs and targets. The temporal exclusion avoids evaluation leakage because interpolated cells can depend on observations from later dates; the official [G02202 v6 User Guide](https://nsidc.org/sites/default/files/documents/user-guide/g02202-v006-userguide.pdf) describes these flags. Weather-filter and spatial-interpolation flags are retained in this initial policy; their impact needs a sensitivity study before scientific model selection.

The API requires a recognized CRS and non-singular GDAL-order affine transform, accepts a strict boolean validity mask, and returns masked cells as `null`, so downstream map clients can retain grid georeferencing and avoid treating land/missing cells as forecasts. Forecast request dimensions are bounded before model execution. It now defaults to the manifest-verified multi-decadal G02202 model and its fingerprint-matched VIIRS transfer report; deployments can override both paths through environment configuration. In production mode it fails closed without a high-entropy service bearer token and explicit browser-origin allowlist; if the token is configured in another mode, it is also enforced, and interactive API documentation/OpenAPI routes are disabled. `/api/*` responses carry an `X-Request-ID`; the service emits JSON access events with request ID, method, path, status, and duration while deliberately omitting bodies, query strings, route geometry, and credentials. A gateway must replace client-supplied request/identity headers and persist actor-attributed immutable audit records correlated by this ID; the app log alone is not an actor audit trail. VIIRS responses are sourced only from manifest-listed NetCDF files; SHA-256 is verified before first indexing and the index is cached until file/manifest metadata changes. The observation catalog also reports the age of its newest mounted timestamp and labels it recent/delayed/stale using clearly identified 48-hour/7-day demonstration thresholds; these are not approved freshness limits and do not assess data quality. These are defense-in-depth and provenance safeguards, not a substitute for ministry OIDC/role authorization, rate limiting, ingress body-size limits, monitoring, or scientific validation. Caller-supplied forecast CRS/source claims are still not independently authenticated, and the model is not operational. See [MINISTRY_INTEGRATION.md](MINISTRY_INTEGRATION.md) for the proposed identity, audit, and operational boundary.

# SCAR historical iceberg-exposure research model (2026-10-02)

The official [SCAR International Iceberg Database](https://doi.org/10.21334/npolar.2021.e4b9a604) was downloaded from the Norwegian Polar Institute's `rel=data` archive link into `work/datasets/scar-icebergs/scar-international-iceberg-database.zip` (SHA-256 `77ec7c73b2c1d1151f6c9854fb3ce4b795013cb3f2e10e2c1a45166f8d834749`). Its nested 2023 archive contains 26,601 NPI and 9,031 AAD ship-observation rows, including observations with zero icebergs. The record states CC BY 4.0. The initial trainer uses only NPI because the Australian records use different size-class protocols and need explicit harmonization before pooling.

`ml/train_scar_exposure.py` fits an empirical-Bayes historical sighting-rate climatology by 5-degree latitude band, 15-degree longitude band, and three-month calendar season. Its target is whether a ship observation recorded at least one iceberg, **not** whether a vessel will encounter an iceberg on a current route. It fits pre-2000 NPI rows and holds out 2000-2010, excluding overlapping cruise IDs from training. The frozen result is `models/scar_historical_exposure.json`; scores and provenance are in `outputs/scar-historical-exposure-holdout.json`. On 5,995 holdout observations the spatial-season model's Brier score is 0.17860 versus 0.18879 for a latitude-season baseline and 0.26171 for a global-rate baseline. The row observations are correlated, unevenly distributed along ship routes, affected by visibility and reporting range, and do not constitute a uniform survey or a calibrated current hazard estimate. This artifact is not wired into the portal's route safety scoring. Reproduce with `work\convlstm-env\Scripts\python.exe -m ml.train_scar_exposure work/datasets/scar-icebergs/scar-international-iceberg-database.zip --output outputs/scar-historical-exposure-holdout.json --model-output models/scar_historical_exposure.json`.
